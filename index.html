BMI Categories

What happens in each BMI range?

BMI is a screening measure. The category describes where your height-to-weight ratio falls; it does not diagnose a health condition.

◌

Underweight

BMI below 18.5

What it means: Weight is below the commonly used adult reference range for your height.

What can happen
  • May reflect inadequate energy or nutrient intake.
  • Some people may have lower muscle or fat stores.
  • Unintentional weight loss deserves attention.
♡

Healthy Weight

BMI 18.5 – 24.9

What it means: BMI falls within the commonly used adult reference range.

What it can suggest
  • A lower BMI-related risk compared with higher ranges.
  • Still does not guarantee good overall health.
  • Nutrition, activity and sleep still matter.
↗

Overweight

BMI 25.0 – 29.9

What it means: Weight is above the commonly used adult reference range for height.

What can happen
  • Health risk can increase as excess body fat increases.
  • Risk varies by waist size, activity and other factors.
  • Small sustainable changes can be useful.
+

Obesity

BMI 30.0 or above

What it means: BMI is in the obesity range used for adults.

What can happen
  • Higher average risk for several chronic conditions.
  • Risk differs substantially between individuals.
  • Professional guidance can help with assessment.

Important accuracy note

For a genuinely more accurate body-fat estimate, a clinician or trained professional can use methods such as skinfold measurements, bioelectrical impedance, or other validated body-composition methods. BMI Buddy's number should be treated as an estimate, not a medical measurement.

FAQ

Common questions

Is BMI Buddy's body-fat number accurate?

It is an estimate. The formula can provide a useful population-level approximation, but it can be noticeably wrong for an individual, especially when body composition differs from average.

Why do you need gender and age?

The adult body-fat equation uses age and sex because body-fat percentage tends to differ across these groups at the same BMI.

Does a healthy BMI mean I am healthy?

No. BMI does not measure fitness, blood pressure, blood sugar, body-fat distribution, muscle mass or many other health factors.

Who should not rely on standard adult BMI categories?

Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific interpretation. Pregnancy also requires a different approach. Athletes with high muscle mass can also be misclassified by BMI.
